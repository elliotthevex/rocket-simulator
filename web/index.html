How this is modeled

Solid propellant is a BATES-style grain feeding a convergent‑divergent nozzle. Burn rate follows Saint Robert’s law r = a·Pcⁿ; chamber pressure is solved from the quasi‑steady balance between propellant burning off the grain and gas leaving the throat — there is no throttle and no shutoff, because there is nothing to command once the grain is lit.

Liquid engines (RP-1, CH₄, LH₂) skip the grain entirely: chamber pressure is a feed-system design choice, scaled live by the throttle you drag during the hotfire, and you can command a cutoff before the tank runs dry. In both cases thrust comes from the same nozzle physics: the real isentropic area‑Mach relation, so climbing to thinner air genuinely increases thrust a little.

Flight is a deliberately simple 1‑D vertical ascent (RK4 integration): constant gravity, an exponential atmosphere, quadratic drag, and a parachute that takes over after apogee. Launch always runs the liquid engine at full throttle. Numbers are illustrative, not certified hardware — defaults mirror the rocket-sim Python project’s example configs.
